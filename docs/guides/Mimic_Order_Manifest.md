## Customs & duty — Canada

**Amazon.ca:** all five items are sold and fulfilled domestically. No customs, no duty, no brokerage. HST added at checkout.

**AliExpress:** three separate charges, often confused.

**1. HST — you will pay this, at checkout.** AliExpress is registered with the CRA and collects Canadian sales tax before you confirm payment. It shows as a "Taxes" or "Estimated Tax" line on the payment screen. At 14% NS HST that's roughly **C$63** on the AliExpress portion. Not a surprise bill at the door.

**2. Customs duty — possible but unlikely.** Canada's duty de minimis is CAD $150 and your AliExpress order is C$449, so it's technically over. Two things work in your favour:

- Most electronic components, modules, and bare boards carry a **0% MFN duty rate** in Canada. The categories in this order are largely duty-free by classification, not by threshold.
- **Your order will split into many packages.** These are 40+ different sellers and warehouses. CBSA assesses each parcel separately, not the order total, so what actually arrives is a series of C$10–60 packages — most below the threshold where anyone looks.

**3. Handling fee — only if duty is assessed.** Canada Post charges a flat C$9.95; private couriers charge C$15–30. Choice items ship through AliExpress's own network, which usually hands off to Canada Post domestically. Where a carrier choice is offered, take Canada Post.

**Realistic expectation:** HST at checkout, and most likely nothing further. Budget C$20–40 as a buffer in case one or two of the larger parcels — the T12 station at C$58.99 is the most likely candidate — gets flagged.

**Worth knowing:** as of 2025, CBSA and other customs authorities run automated valuation checks against AliExpress's public listing prices. If a seller offers to declare a lower value or mark a package as a gift, decline — mismatches trigger reassessment plus penalties.

## First jobs when the boxes land

1. **Label as you unpack.** Twenty minutes now versus an afternoon in three weeks trying to tell an LM2596 from an MP1584 by sight.
2. **Check the AS5600 magnet is diametric** — spin it near the sensor; the angle output should sweep smoothly through 360°. Jumps or sticks means the wrong magnet type.
3. **Check the JCD stand's bore fits the 907 handle.**
4. **Identify the motor's 6 wires** — the two thicker ones read a few ohms to each other on the multimeter (motor power). The other four are encoder VCC / GND / A / B.
5. **Measure counts-per-output-revolution.** Mark the shaft, turn it exactly 10 times by hand, read the counter, divide by 10. Your motor has no datasheet — this is your first real experiment, and it's the number your whole control loop depends on.
